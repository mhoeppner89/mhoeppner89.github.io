Models in Decision-Making Under Risk and Uncertainty

Explore the example

One choice, many lenses

The same (fictional) decision can look different depending on what a model treats as important.

The decision

Would you buy flood insurance?

You own a house worth €300,000. A serious flood could cause about €50,000 in damage. Insurance costs €420 per year. Historical data suggest an annual flood probability of roughly 1%, but climate change makes that estimate uncertain.

€420 annual premium
€50,000 potential damage
≈1% estimated annual probability

Buy insurance

Pay €420 each year; a serious flood is covered.

Skip insurance

Pay no premium; feel risky.

Select a lens

Selected lens

Expected Utility Theory

Normative benchmark

Compares the utility of having your current wealth minus the €420 for protection for sure with the weighted sum of the utilities of keeping all your wealth and your wealth minus €50,000 in case of the flood.

How it reads the choice
If the probability and damage estimate are credible, the decision depends on the probability point estimate (1%) and how you value your wealth.
What it leaves open
It does not explain why a rare loss may feel more important than its probability. It also usually assumes a very specific utility curve that implies risk aversion.
